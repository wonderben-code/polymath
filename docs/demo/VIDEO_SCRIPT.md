# Polymath demo film: narration and shot list

Three minutes, built from the live screen recordings of 2 November 2026 (see `README.md` in this folder). Two cuts exist from the same shot list and the same narration: **A, the best-bits cut**, which jumps to the strongest moment of each feature and holds on the result; and **B, the unfold cut**, where each feature runs continuously from the first keystroke to the result with speed ramps instead of jumps, so the circle, the map and the stages are seen forming while the narrator speaks. The narration times are the same in both, give or take two seconds.

Read at a natural pace (about 115 words a minute). Each block is timed to its segment; a little silence at the end of a block is fine.

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | Title card: *Polymath. Discoveries hide between fields of science. Polymath finds them.* | Science is split into thousands of specialties. An answer found in one field often stays unknown in the field that needs it. |
| 0:06–0:14 | The home page | Polymath fuses Meta's Muse Spark with an engine that reads across the open record of world science. Meta brings the reasoning and the creativity. Polymath brings the literature, the evidence checks, and the memory. |
| 0:14–0:36 | **Ask.** The question is typed, the run unfolds, then the answer: numbered claims with their labels and source passages, and "what remains unanswered". | Ask it a question. Polymath plans the searches, reads real papers from arXiv and OpenAlex, and answers with numbered claims. Each claim is labelled known, postulated or speculative, and every one is backed by the exact passages it came from. It also says what remains unanswered. |
| 0:36–1:00 | **Solve.** The problem is typed, the run unfolds, then the lock, the key that was found, the fits from other fields, and "A new key was forged". | Solve takes a problem. First Meta looks for an answer anywhere in science. Then it tries fits from other fields, here hydrogen-rich materials from storage research, for cosmic-ray shielding. When no single fit is enough, it forges a new key by combining the fits that held, and says plainly where it falls short. |
| 1:00–1:36 | **Converge.** The theme is typed; the empty circle of twelve fields; connections form; the synthesis glows in the centre; "Paused at your spending ceiling". | Converge draws a circle of distant fields around one theme. Meta chooses the pairs, tests each connection against the literature, and keeps only what survives its strongest objection. The connections that hold are drawn inward, and a deeper synthesis forms in the centre. Every step has a budget, and the circle stops itself at the visitor's spending ceiling. |
| 1:36–2:00 | **Frontier.** The question is typed; the map grows from one node to gaps and a six-step plan; the programme is paused. | Frontier takes on an open problem. It surveys the literature, names the gaps, looks for solutions in other fields, and breaks the stubborn gaps into steps, mapping the path from question to answer. This one grew a six-step plan for a space elevator in nineteen minutes. |

Give it a brief and your requirements. Meta hunts far fields for mechanisms that already work, here humpback tubercles, owl serrations and seal stroke economy, and combines them into something new: a sawtooth buoyancy glider-submarine. Meta Muse Image paints the concept art. Then it engineers the design with cited numbers, stress-tests it against your own requirements, and lists what is still to invent, each gap one click from Solve. Every source is real, and it says what it could not prove. |

Polymath: discoveries between fields, found with Meta. |

## Recording the voiceover

1. Open the draft cut and the table above side by side.
2. Record each block as the segment plays (phone voice memo or any recorder is fine; a quiet room matters more than the microphone).
3. Send the audio file; it is laid over the picture, trimmed to the segment starts, and the final MP4 is rendered.
