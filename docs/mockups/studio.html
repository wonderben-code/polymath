Hush: a rotorcraft that flies like an owl

Three ideas that already work in nature, combined into one rotor. Each piece below links to the exact passage it came from.

Where the ideas came from

Ornithology → blade edgeComb-like leading edge

Owls' serrated feather edges break up the airflow that makes wing noise. Applied to each rotor blade's leading edge.

“…[exact saved passage from a real paper]…”
Entomology → blade shapeFolded, corrugated blade

Dragonfly wings are pleated, stiff and light. Proposed for a lighter blade cross-section.

Botany → safetySeed-like autorotation

A maple seed spins to fall slowly. Proposed as a passive, controlled descent if power fails.

SpecVerdictWhy

Safe if power failsPlausibleAutorotation is standard for helicopters; the seed-inspired blade twist is untested.

The strongest objection

Scale. A dragonfly wing works at a size and speed where air behaves very differently from a full-size rotor. The fold that helps an insect may only add drag on a helicopter blade.

Verdict: keep the owl edge and the autorotation; treat the corrugated blade as unproven until a scaled test.

Prior art check

Serrated rotor and fan edges have been studied; the three-part combination was not found.

Still to invent

  • A serration pattern that survives rain erosion
  • Proof that the folds help at full scale
