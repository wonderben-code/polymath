## What the organisers say about the video and submission

From the participant guide (https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/meta-ai-developer-hackathon/details), section 7, "Submission Process":

> Please double-check that your repository is accessible publicly, your video link is accessible, and everyone who worked on the project is listed on the submission page.

> Please note: Your submission will require a three minute demo video highlighting the specific features, code, and functionality you built during the Hackathon. This video will also be used as one of the primary artifacts Judges use to judge your project, along with your code and live product.

The form's video field expects a link (placeholder "https://youtube.com/watch?v=..."). There is no upload field, so the film must be hosted (for example YouTube, public or unlisted) and the link must open for anyone.

From the organisers' email of 3 Nov 2026:

> Spend time on your 3-minute demo video. Showcasing your project well is critical in the judging process.
> Ensure your github is open source. This technical detail is mandatory for all projects.

## Rules that affect the answers (participant guide, section 4)

> Demo requirements: Your demo must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built during the Hackathon. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at Judges sole discretion.

> New work only: What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in your submissions.

## Judging (participant guide, section 8)

Stage One is pass/fail: the project "reasonably fits the theme and reasonably used Meta Model API and/or Meta's open-weight models". Stage Two scores 0–10 on:

| Category (weight) | Description |
|---|---|
| Impact (30%) | What's the real-world potential here? Who benefits, and how much does it matter? Could this actually become something people use? Does it fit into one of the problem statements listed above? |
| Use of Meta AI Tooling (25%) | How creatively did this team use Meta's AI tooling? Did they go beyond a basic integration? Did they surface capabilities that surprised even us? |
| Technical Depth & Execution (20%) | Is the engineering sound and thoughtfully refined? Does this feel like something that was thought through with real craft. |
| Demo (25%) | Is this a working, impressive demo? Does the product hold up live? Is the demo video genuinely cool to watch? |

Stage Three: the top 5 per track go to a final round where judges watch the video demos live and rank them with the same rubric.

Indie Developer track: "For individual builders and students. Judged on creativity, technical ambition, and whether it works."

Bonus prizes that may be relevant: Most Viral Project (X post link in question 5, engagement until 8 Nov), Best Agentic Use-Case, Best Creative Build (Muse Image or Muse Wonder), Community Vote.
