Discoveries hide between fields of science. Polymath finds them.

Science is split into thousands of specialties, so an answer found in one field often stays unknown in the field that needs it. Polymath gives Meta's Muse Spark the open record of world science to read, lets it decide where to look, and checks every claim against the actual papers.

The answer you need may already exist, in a field you never read.

Far more is published every year than anyone could read. The great leaps that joined fields depended on rare people who happened to know both. Polymath makes that search systematic: Meta's model chooses which fields to look in, what to read next, and what to believe; Polymath's engine supplies the literature, checks every quotation, and remembers.

It reads arXiv and OpenAlex, opens the full text of arXiv papers where it matters, and only cites text it actually retrieved and saved.

Five tools

For any question, problem or invention.

Each one is a long, live investigation that Meta's model directs step by step. You watch it think: every search, reading, hypothesis and setback streams as it happens.

Ask

A question, answered with the exact evidence.

Type any open question. Muse Spark plans the searches, reads real papers and answers with numbered claims.

  • Every claim is labelled Known, Postulated or Speculative.
  • Each one shows the exact passage it came from, matched against the saved source.
  • It says plainly what remains unanswered.

Real run: "Why does time only run forward?" answered in under two minutes, with every claim tied to its passage.

Solve

A problem, solved with answers from other fields.

Describe a problem: the lock. Polymath first looks for a key anywhere in science.

  • If none fits, it finds the closest fits in other fields and says exactly where each one falls short.
  • Then it forges a new key by combining the fits that held, tested against the original evidence.
  • It tells you honestly where the new proposal is still weak.

Real run: "How could a spacecraft shield its crew from cosmic rays without heavy metal?" A key was forged from hydrogen-rich materials used in energy storage, in eleven minutes.

Converge

One theme, a circle of distant fields.

Give it any topic you're curious about. Muse Spark picks far-apart fields, from twelve up to two hundred, and asks what each one knows.

  • It tests every connection against the literature and tries hard to break it.
  • Only connections that survive their strongest objection are drawn inward.
  • What survives forms a deeper idea in the centre, level after level.

Real run: "How systems remember" across twelve fields converged on memory as hidden-state history, linking statistical mechanics, ecology, machine learning and climate science.

Frontier

An unsolved problem, mapped into a research programme.

Give it a problem nobody has solved, however sci-fi.

  • It surveys the literature and names the gaps between today and the goal.
  • It hunts other sciences for answers to each gap.
  • It breaks the hardest gaps into steps, each labelled by its evidence; any step can be sent to Solve.

Real run: "How could we build a space elevator with materials we can make today?" grew a six-step plan in nineteen minutes.

Studio

A brief, turned into an invention.

Describe anything you want to create, and your requirements.

  • Muse Spark turns the brief into the functions it needs and hunts far fields for mechanisms that already achieve each one.
  • It invents a design from them, engineers it with worked calculations and cited numbers, and stress-tests it against your requirements.
  • Meta Muse Image paints the concept art: the design in its world, a cutaway, the key mechanism up close.
  • It lists what is still to invent, each gap one click from Solve.

Real run: "A submarine inspired by whales" combined humpback fin bumps, owl feather serrations and seal swimming economy into a submarine that glides on a sawtooth path.

Truth over plausibility

Nothing is surfaced without real evidence and a serious attempt to break it.

  • A finding can only quote text Polymath actually retrieved and saved, and every quotation is matched against the saved source.
  • Every connection carries the strongest objection the model could find, its verdict on it, and a search for prior work.
  • Claims are labelled by how well they are supported, and the limits of each search are stated.
  • An honest "not supported" is a valid result. Polymath never invents papers or sources.

Use Polymath

Run your own Polymath, on your own Meta key.

Polymath is open source. Your own copy has every tool and feature on this page, including concept art and Zenodo publishing. Your Meta key stays on your own server and is never shared with anyone, including us.

  1. Get a Meta Model API key from Meta's developer dashboard and set a spending limit there.
  2. Get a small server (2 GB is enough) and point a web address at it.
  3. Run one setup command. It installs everything and makes every secret on the server itself.
  4. Connect your Meta key with one more command. It asks for your key and your spending limits, makes one check call and switches research on.
  5. Optional: switch on Zenodo publishing and sign-in with one more command.

Questions

Why can't I paste my Meta key into this website?

Meta's terms don't allow sharing your Model API key with another service. Running your own copy keeps your key on your own server, which is ordinary use of your own key.

Do I need to be a programmer?

No. The guide is written for non-programmers: you copy three commands into your server and answer a few questions. Expect about half an hour the first time.

Which Meta models does it use?

Muse Spark for research and reasoning by default, and Muse Image for Studio's concept art. You can switch to Muse Wonder or any newer Meta model with one setting.

Can I control what it spends?

Yes. You set a total and a daily cap; every call is reserved against them before it is sent, and one command stops all spending at once. Set a spending limit on your Meta account too.

Where do my findings go?

Everything is saved on your own server. You choose what to share publicly and what to publish to your own Zenodo account.

Is the science reliable?

Polymath labels every claim by its support, quotes only text it retrieved, and attaches the strongest objection to every connection. It is a research partner, not a replacement for expert review: treat findings as well-sourced leads.
